# Swipe gesture and thumbstick practice preview

Approved experimental preview, not a production rules change. Compare discrete swipe gestures with a floating thumbstick; toggle stamina independently. A recognised quick swipe produces one finite movement burst with quick acceleration and a short glide. Held/slow dragging does not steer. Existing game modes, profiles, scores and recovery stay unchanged.

## Decisions

- DEV-only SMF route `?rehearsal&controls`; one reusable Phaser game, existing six venue floors and crowd rules.
- Owner's latest correction: the floor acts as a directional pad. A continuous completed swipe launches on release in its screen-space direction, regardless of player/contact position. Use the whole stroke, not its early segment; pauses and sharp backtracking invalidate the contact, with tolerance for natural wobble. One burst per contact; the next swipe replaces the prior burst; a tap brakes. Thumbstick has a separate hold-to-sprint button. Keyboard supports arrows/WASD plus Shift.
- Ordinary movement is always available. Extra speed spends stamina; normal movement/rest recovers it. Stamina off permits the same sprint ceiling without consumption. Both styles share the same model and limits.
- Recognition uses CSS pixels: at least 18px within 350ms, average speed ≥0.3px/ms, net displacement ≥85% of stroke path. Reject gaps over 120ms without meaningful progress; accumulate 4px progress so tiny jitter cannot hide hesitation and high-frequency sampling still works. Use one native event clock, including coalesced samples, so browser dispatch delays do not change the gesture. Sensitivity scales distance/speed thresholds. All thresholds remain provisional phone-feel tuning.
- Completed stroke length independently selects 300–500ms driven duration (smooth curve between 18–180px), and average velocity selects speed 300–480 (smooth curve between 0.3–1.5px/ms). Sensitivity scales both length and velocity; Sprint overrides speed only. Existing quick ramp, ≤120ms glide, stamina limits and terrain remain. Equal complete gestures produce equal trips despite different event sampling. No camera-scale multiplication or player-relative aiming. The dance floor is the sole steering surface; full-floor framing remains the lab baseline. Thumbstick Sprint sits in a compact lower corner opposite the selected steering hand, clear of Bass Head at the top.
- Pause, visibility loss, pointer cancellation, resize, mode/venue changes clear active input. Pause freezes motion and stamina. No persistence or service clients in the entry.
- Candy is disabled in this focused comparison to avoid surprise changes to input/speed. Existing mud, bass and Bass Head remain available; fresh rounds use reproducible spark placement.
